# The Psychology of Impulse Spending

Most overspending is not a maths problem; it is a behaviour problem. Apps, sales and social media are designed to make buying effortless.

## Why we overspend

- **Frictionless payments**: UPI and saved cards make spending feel painless, so small amounts do not register.
- **FOMO and social pressure**: trips, parties and gadgets your friends buy, and influencer content, push "wants" into feeling like "needs".
- **Scarcity tricks**: "only 2 left", countdown timers and flash sales rush decisions.
- **Emotional spending**: buying when stressed, bored, lonely or celebrating, especially late at night.
- **Mental accounting**: treating birthday money, cashback or refunds as "free money".

## Practical techniques

- **The 24-hour rule**: wait a day before any non-essential purchase above a set amount, say ₹1,000. For bigger items, wait a week.
- **Remove saved cards** and log out of shopping apps; extra steps reduce impulse buys.
- **Unsubscribe and mute**: sale emails, app notifications and accounts that make you want to buy.
- **Use a "fun money" budget**: a fixed weekly amount you can spend guilt-free. When it is gone, it is gone.
- **Cost in hours**: translate a price into hours of work or days of pocket money.
- **Shop with a list**, both online and offline.
- **Track and review weekly**: seeing a category total in BudgetIt is often enough to change behaviour.

## Handling social pressure

Suggest cheaper plans (potluck, free events, cafes instead of clubs), be honest that you are saving for a goal, and decline some plans without guilt. Good friends understand.

## Be kind to yourself

One slip does not ruin a budget. Note what triggered it and adjust; consistency over months matters more than a perfect week.

# Budgeting Methods for Students: 50/30/20 and Zero-Based

A budget is simply a plan for your money before the month starts. Two methods work well for students and first-jobbers in India.

## The 50/30/20 rule

Split your monthly income (pocket money, stipend, part-time pay or salary) into three buckets:

- **50% needs**: rent or PG/hostel fees, mess or groceries, transport to college or office, phone recharge, utilities.
- **30% wants**: eating out, Zomato/Swiggy orders, movies, OTT subscriptions, shopping, trips.
- **20% savings**: emergency fund, goals (laptop, trip, course fee), investments such as an RD or SIP.

Example on ₹15,000 a month: about ₹7,500 for needs, ₹4,500 for wants and ₹3,000 for savings. If rent alone eats most of your money (common in metros), adjust to something like 60/25/15 and keep saving *something* every month.

## Zero-based budgeting

Give every rupee a job until income minus planned spending equals zero. List income, then assign amounts to each category (rent, food, transport, entertainment, utilities, savings, other). Savings is a category too, so "zero" does not mean spending everything.

Zero-based budgeting suits irregular income such as freelancing or internships, because you re-plan each month with what actually came in.

## Making it stick

- Pay yourself first: move savings to a separate account or RD on the day money arrives.
- Track every expense for the first month, even ₹20 chai. Small UPI payments add up fast.
- Use weekly limits for food and entertainment; a monthly limit is easy to blow in week one.
- Review at month end: which category overshot, and why? Adjust next month's plan instead of giving up.

*This is educational information, not financial advice.*

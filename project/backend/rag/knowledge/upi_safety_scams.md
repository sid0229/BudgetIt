# UPI Safety and Common Payment Scams

UPI is fast and convenient, which is exactly why fraudsters target it. A few rules prevent almost all UPI fraud.

## The golden rules

- **You never need your UPI PIN to receive money.** Entering your PIN always means money is leaving your account. If someone asks for your UPI PIN, or asks you to enter it to "receive" a payment, it is a scam.
- **Never share your UPI PIN, OTP, CVV or card PIN** with anyone, including people claiming to be from your bank, NPCI, RBI, police, a courier or a customer care team.
- **Do not scan QR codes to receive money.** Scanning a QR code and entering your PIN sends money.
- **Check "collect requests" carefully.** A collect request asks you to pay. Decline requests you did not expect.

## Common scams targeting students and young people

- **Fake buyer on OLX/marketplace**: "buyer" sends a QR code or collect request saying it will pay you. It actually debits you.
- **Wrong transfer trick**: someone claims they sent money by mistake and pressures you to send it back. Check your actual bank balance, not a screenshot or SMS.
- **Fake customer care numbers** found through search results or social media, who ask you to install screen-sharing apps like AnyDesk or TeamViewer.
- **Part-time job / task scams**: "like videos and earn", then pay a "deposit" to unlock bigger earnings.
- **Fake rental listings** asking for a token advance before you visit.
- **KYC update / electricity disconnection SMS** with a link or a number to call.
- **"Digital arrest" calls** from fake police or officials demanding money. Real agencies do not arrest people over video calls or ask for payments.

## Protect your account

- Set a strong, private UPI PIN and app lock; do not use your birth year.
- Install payment apps only from official app stores.
- Turn on transaction SMS/email alerts and check them.
- Lower your daily UPI limit in the app if you do not need a high limit.

## If you are defrauded

Act immediately; speed matters.

1. Call the national cybercrime helpline **1930**, and report at **cybercrime.gov.in**.
2. Call your bank's official helpline (from the bank website or the back of your card) to block the account, card or UPI.
3. Report the transaction as fraud in your UPI app and keep screenshots, transaction IDs and the fraudster's number or UPI ID.

*This is educational information, not financial advice.*

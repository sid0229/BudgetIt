# Avoiding Debt Traps: BNPL, Instant Loan Apps and EMIs

Easy credit is everywhere: Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) at checkout, "no-cost EMI" on phones, and instant loan apps on social media. Used carelessly they lead to debt that follows you for years.

## Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL)

- BNPL is a loan, even if it feels like a payment option. It is reported to credit bureaus and late payments can hurt your credit score.
- Late fees and penalties can be steep. Read the terms before using it.
- Multiple BNPL accounts make it easy to lose track; use at most one, and only for planned purchases you could pay for today.

## "No-cost EMI"

The interest is often built into the price or offset by a discount you would otherwise get, and processing fees may apply. Compare the total amount you will pay against the upfront price.

## Instant loan apps

- Borrow only from banks, RBI-regulated NBFCs or apps linked to them. RBI's Sachet portal (sachet.rbi.org.in) helps you check entities and report illegal lending or deposit schemes.
- Illegal loan apps demand access to your contacts and photos, charge huge hidden fees, and harass borrowers and their contacts.
- If you are harassed, report it at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930, and file a complaint with the police. Do not pay more money under threat.

## Warning signs you are in a debt trap

- Taking a new loan to pay an old one.
- Paying only the minimum due on credit cards.
- Not knowing your total outstanding amount.
- Hiding borrowing from family.

## Getting out

1. List every debt: lender, amount, interest/fees, due date.
2. Stop new borrowing and pause non-essential spending.
3. Pay the most expensive debt first while paying minimums on others.
4. Talk to your lender about restructuring if you cannot pay; ignoring calls makes it worse.
5. Ask a trusted family member or a registered financial counsellor for help.

*This is educational information, not financial advice.*

# Income Tax Basics for Your First Job

Getting your first salary also means dealing with income tax. Tax slabs, limits and deductions change in Union Budgets, so always confirm current numbers on incometax.gov.in.

## Documents you will deal with

- **PAN**: needed for your salary account, investments and filing returns. Link it with Aadhaar.
- **Salary slip and CTC**: CTC is not your take-home pay. It includes employer PF contribution, gratuity, insurance and variable pay.
- **Form 16**: issued by your employer after the financial year, showing salary paid and TDS deducted.
- **Form 26AS and AIS (Annual Information Statement)**: available on the income tax portal; they show TDS, interest income and other transactions reported against your PAN. Check them before filing.

## Old regime vs new regime

- The new tax regime is the default; you can choose the old regime if it is better for you.
- The new regime has lower slab rates but allows few deductions.
- The old regime allows deductions such as Section 80C (EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance), 80D (health insurance), HRA and home loan interest.
- Tell your employer your choice at the start of the year (for TDS) and compare both before filing. The income tax portal has a tax calculator.

## Filing your Income Tax Return (ITR)

- Most salaried individuals file ITR-1 or ITR-2 online at incometax.gov.in.
- The usual due date for individuals not requiring an audit is 31 July after the financial year ends, unless extended; check the portal.
- File even if TDS has been deducted, so you can claim refunds, carry losses forward and have proof of income for loans and visas.
- E-verify your return (Aadhaar OTP or net banking) after filing; an unverified return is not complete.

## Common first-job mistakes

- Ignoring interest from savings accounts and FDs; it is taxable and appears in AIS.
- Not submitting investment and rent proofs to the employer on time, leading to higher TDS.
- Withdrawing EPF when switching jobs instead of transferring it.

## Beware of tax scams

The Income Tax Department does not ask for PINs, passwords or card details by email, SMS or phone. Refund-related messages with links are a common phishing trick.

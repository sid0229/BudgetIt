# Building an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money kept aside only for genuine surprises: a medical bill, a broken phone or laptop you need for work, a sudden trip home, or a gap between jobs or internships.

## How much to keep

- **Students**: start with a small target such as one month of essential expenses (rent/PG, food, transport). Even ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 prevents panic borrowing.
- **Working professionals**: a common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. People with dependants or unstable income should aim higher.

Calculate essentials only (rent, food, utilities, transport, EMIs, insurance), not your full lifestyle spend.

## Where to keep it

The fund must be safe and quick to access, not chase returns:

- A separate savings account (not the one linked to your daily UPI apps), so you do not spend it by accident.
- A sweep-in or flexi fixed deposit, or a short FD you can break if needed.
- Liquid mutual funds are used by some people for part of the fund, but understand the small market risk and withdrawal time first.

Avoid locking emergency money in long lock-in products like PPF, or in stocks that may be down exactly when you need cash.

## How to build it

1. Set a target and a monthly amount, e.g. ₹1,000 a month.
2. Automate the transfer on the day you receive money.
3. Add windfalls: birthday money, scholarship surplus, festival bonus.
4. If you use it, pause other goals and refill it first.

## What is not an emergency

A sale, a concert, a new phone model or a trip with friends. Plan those as separate savings goals.

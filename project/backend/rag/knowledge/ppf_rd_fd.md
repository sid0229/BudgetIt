# PPF, Recurring Deposits and Fixed Deposits Basics

These are low-risk savings options popular in India. Interest rates change from time to time, so always check the current rate on the official website before investing.

## Fixed Deposit (FD)

- Deposit a lump sum for a fixed period (from days to years) at an interest rate fixed when you open it.
- Good for money you need on a known date, such as next semester's fees.
- Breaking an FD early usually means a penalty or lower interest.
- Bank deposits are insured by DICGC up to a limit per depositor per bank; check dicgc.org.in for the current limit.
- Interest is taxable as per your income slab.

## Recurring Deposit (RD)

- Deposit a fixed amount every month, for example ₹1,000, for a chosen period at a fixed rate.
- Ideal for students building a habit or saving for a specific goal like a laptop or trip within one to three years.
- Available at banks and at post offices (Post Office RD). Missing instalments can attract penalties.

## Public Provident Fund (PPF)

- A government-backed long-term savings scheme available through post offices and authorised banks.
- It has a 15-year maturity (lock-in) period with limited partial withdrawals and loan facilities after some years, so it is for long-term goals, not emergencies.
- There are minimum and maximum annual deposit limits, and the interest rate is set by the government each quarter.
- Under the old tax regime, deposits can qualify for deduction under Section 80C, and interest and maturity amount are tax-free under current rules. Check incometax.gov.in for the latest position.

## Other small savings schemes

Post office schemes such as the National Savings Certificate (NSC), Time Deposit and Monthly Income Scheme are listed with current rates on indiapost.gov.in and nsiindia.gov.in.

## Choosing between them

- Need money within a year or two: FD or RD.
- Long-term, safe, tax-efficient saving: PPF.
- Long-term growth with market risk: consider equity mutual funds (see the SIP guide).

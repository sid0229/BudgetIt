# Renting a Room, PG or Flat as a Student or First-Jobber

Rent is usually your largest fixed expense, so getting it right saves more than any amount of cutting coffee.

## How much rent is reasonable

Try to keep rent and housing (including PG food charges if included) to roughly a third of your monthly income or allowance. In expensive metros this may not be possible; then cut elsewhere and share accommodation.

## Before you pay a deposit

- **Visit in person** or have someone you trust visit. Never pay a "token amount" to hold a room you have only seen in photos; fake rental listings are a common scam.
- **Read the agreement**: rent, security deposit, notice period, lock-in, electricity billing, maintenance, and what happens to the deposit when you leave.
- **Get a rent agreement and receipts.** Salaried tenants may need rent receipts and the landlord's PAN (above certain rent levels) to claim HRA under the old tax regime; check incometax.gov.in for current rules.
- **Photograph the room** on move-in to avoid deposit deductions for old damage.

## PG vs shared flat

- **PG/hostel**: predictable cost, food and cleaning often included, less hassle. Check meal quality, curfew and what is extra (laundry, AC, Wi-Fi).
- **Shared flat**: often cheaper per person and more freedom, but you manage groceries, cook, maid, electricity and internet bills. Use a shared expense tracker and split bills monthly.

## Lowering ongoing costs

- Share Wi-Fi and OTT plans with flatmates.
- Track the electricity meter; AC and geysers drive bills up.
- Negotiate rent at renewal, especially if you have paid on time.
- Living near a metro or your college/office can save more on transport than a cheaper room far away.

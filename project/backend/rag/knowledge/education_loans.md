# Education Loan Basics

An education loan can fund tuition, hostel, books, a laptop and sometimes travel for study in India or abroad. It is a serious long-term commitment, so understand it before signing.

## Key terms to understand

- **Moratorium period**: usually the course duration plus a period after it, during which full EMIs are not required. Interest often still accrues during this time; paying the interest during your studies (if you can) reduces the final burden.
- **Collateral and co-applicant**: smaller loans may not need collateral; larger ones often need property or deposits as security, and usually a parent or guardian as co-borrower.
- **Interest rate type**: floating rates can change over the loan term. Compare the current rate, processing fees and prepayment terms directly on lenders' official websites.
- **Margin money**: the part of the cost you pay yourself.

## Where to compare and apply

- The government PM-Vidyalaxmi portal lets students compare and apply for education loans from multiple banks and check eligible government interest subsidy schemes.
- Public sector banks, private banks and NBFCs all offer education loans; read the sanction letter fully.

## Tax benefit

Interest paid on an education loan for higher studies may be deductible under Section 80E of the Income Tax Act under the old regime, for a limited number of years. Check incometax.gov.in for current rules and whether your chosen tax regime allows it.

## Borrow responsibly

- Borrow only what you need; estimate likely starting salary in your field before choosing the amount.
- Keep all documents, statements and the repayment schedule.
- Missing EMIs damages your CIBIL score and affects your co-applicant too.
- Beware agents who promise guaranteed approval for a fee.

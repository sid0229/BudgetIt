# Saving on Food in Hostels, PGs and Shared Flats

Food is usually the biggest flexible expense for students and young professionals. Food delivery from Zomato and Swiggy, quick-commerce snacks from Blinkit, Zepto or Instamart, and canteen runs can quietly double your food spending.

## Know where the money goes

- Check your food spending for the last four weeks and split it into mess/groceries, eating out, and delivery.
- Delivery often costs much more than the menu price once you add delivery fees, platform fees, packaging, taxes and tips.
- Late-night orders and "small" ₹150 snack orders are the most common leaks.

## Practical ways to cut food costs

- **Use the mess or PG meals you already pay for.** Skipping a prepaid meal and then ordering in means paying twice.
- **Set a weekly delivery limit**, e.g. two orders a week or ₹600, and track it in BudgetIt.
- **Cook simple meals in a group.** Sharing groceries for rice, dal, eggs, poha, oats or sandwiches with flatmates lowers per-person cost.
- **Buy staples in bulk** from a local kirana, wholesale market or monthly grocery sale, not daily from quick-commerce apps.
- **Carry snacks and a water bottle** to college or office to avoid impulse canteen and cafe spends.
- **Turn off delivery app notifications** and remove saved cards to add friction.
- **Use memberships only if you order often enough** to recover the fee; otherwise cancel.

## Eating out smartly

- Prefer lunch deals and thalis over dinner a-la-carte.
- Split bills fairly using a UPI split feature or a shared expense tracker.
- Treat cafes as a "want" in your 50/30/20 budget, not a daily habit.

## A simple weekly food plan

Plan meals on Sunday, shop once, cook in batches, and allow one or two planned treats. Planned treats feel better and cost less than random cravings.

*This is educational information, not financial advice.*
